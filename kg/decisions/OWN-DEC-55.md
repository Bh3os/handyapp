Owner wording (project chat, 2026-10-12): "Newsreader + Source Sans 3: Approve. Excellent balance of editorial luxury and practical readability." "Replace work-order ticket with plate + At a glance: Approve. More refined, less industrial." "I'd specify Newsreader for prominent marketing headlines and selected section titles, with Source Sans 3 everywhere else, including navigation, service cards, form inputs, small labels and the customer dashboard. Avoid using Newsreader at tiny sizes, and don't overuse italic headline treatments. Premium typography depends on restraint." "Retire the work-order ticket visual from the customer-facing website. Use the refined service plate and clean 'At a glance' information rows. Preserve functional work-order interfaces in the internal admin and technician portals. Prioritize service photography, relevant details, and clear estimate actions over decorative UI elements." "The key is to make 'At a glance' genuinely informative rather than filling it with decorative labels or unverified service promises." "Every customer-facing element should reinforce HandyHub's premium home-improvement identity: thoughtful, polished, trustworthy, approachable, and operationally reliable."

Effect:
- Newsreader is used only for prominent marketing headlines and selected section titles, never below 24 px and never italic.
- Source Sans 3 is used everywhere else: navigation, service cards, form inputs, small labels, specification values, the customer dashboard and the staff app.
- The work-order ticket visual is removed from the customer website and kept in the internal work-order screens.
- Service pages use the service plate and factual "At a glance" rows (Services, Estimate, Service area, Visits, Next step); no decorative labels or unverified promises.
- Both typefaces are self-hosted.

Purpose (SOP section 1):

Install new decorative trim: crown molding where the ceiling dips or waves (Option A) and wainscoting box panels on flat walls (Option B), cut, fitted and fastened so that joints are tight appropriate to the finish level, pieces are securely attached, reveals and alignment are consistent, holes and seams are filled or caulked per the approved scope, and the surface is ready for the agreed finish (source: spec Appendix B HI-09; spec HI-09.QAC; original Option A and B headings in section 12). Painting is not part of this service unless a separate painting agreement covers it (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause). This draft authorizes no work and promises no result to the customer (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; 31 section 3.5 rule 5).

Standard builder-grade baseboards and bare drywall corners can make even high-end rooms look incomplete. Installing custom crown molding, deep baseboards, window casings, or elegant wainscoting panels adds instant architectural character and upscale value to any residence. Our finish carpentry team specializes in executing flawless, micro-tight wood joints that turn ordinary walls into stunning custom showcases.

- [ ] **Wall Contour Level Mapping:** We inspect wall plumbness and ceiling flat lines, calculating specialized wood backing shims to compensate for standard room variations.
- [ ] **Precision Compound Miter Cutting:** We cut fine finish lumber on heavy-duty miter saws, utilizing compound angles to ensure inside and outside corners lock tight without gaps.
- [ ] **Pneumatic Brad Fastening:** We secure the trim pieces onto framing lines using non-marring pneumatic brad nailers, counter-sinking heads cleanly below the wood surface.
- [ ] **Seamless Nail Hole Filling:** We pack all fastener dimples with premium wood putty, caulk the long linear wall boundaries, and sand joints smooth for flawless paint prep.

## Included work (SOP section 2, "In scope")

- Option A: crown molding on ceilings that dip or wave: compound miter cutting, shimming, brad nailing into wood framing, then trimming the shims, caulking the ceiling edge and filling brad holes (source: section 12, Option A).
- Option B: wainscoting box panels on flat walls: chalk-line grid layout, construction adhesive, pin nailing and caulking of inside joints (source: section 12, Option B).
- Paint preparation limited to the agreement's inclusions: "mechanical installation, brad counter-sinking, wood putty filling, and continuous edge caulking" (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause).

### SOP section 2, "Do not perform"

1. Electrical, plumbing, gas or HVAC work outside the approved scope or requiring a licensed trade. Reason: licensed trade. Instead: stop and escalate (source: spec START-011 "unapproved electrical/plumbing/gas/structural scope"; master section 1.3).
2. Structural work, meaning cutting, removing, sistering or adding load-bearing members, ledgers, footings or framing, unless it is in the approved scope with the required qualified review. Reason: structural. Instead: stop and escalate (source: spec START-011; master section 1.3).
3. Disturbing suspect asbestos, lead paint or mold. This includes removing, cutting or sanding existing painted trim or other painted surfaces in a home built before 1978 or of unknown build year until a qualified person records a disposition; whether the EPA RRP rule applies is not concluded here (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; X-13; risk-triage playbook "A pre-1978 painted surface is not cleared by a customer saying it looks safe"; owner facts 2026-10-02 "Unknown is not safe"). Reason: hazardous material. Suspected asbestos / lead paint / mold: stop and escalate (source: spec START-011).
4. Work that is not in the approved scope or an approved change order, including baseboards, casings, sanding or removal of existing trim until the owner approves them (section 2). Reason: out of scope. Instead: record the customer's request for a change order (source: spec JOB-005, JOB-010, FIN-008, AT-033).
5. Working at height or using access equipment beyond the approved height and access policy. No such policy exists yet (02 G-005), so crown work and any other work that needs a ladder is held until it does (31 section 8.4 rule 4, PROPOSED). Reason: policy. Instead: stop and escalate (source: HI-06.SAF "beyond approved height/access policy"; DW-04.TLS "scaffolds/stilts only under approved policy"; spec HI-09.HAZ "ladder"; HI-09.SAF "ladder controls").
6. Mixing or sequencing chemicals in any way the approved step and the product SDS do not allow. Reason: hazardous material. Instead: stop and escalate (source: CL-01.SAF "never mix incompatible chemicals"; X-09).
7. Recording, photographing or sending customer passwords, master codes or site access codes outside the app's secured fields. Reason: policy (privacy). Instead: use the app's secured fields only (source: spec COM-002; owner facts 2026-10-02; 27 Roles and permissions AUTH-009).
8. Promising results, warranties, safety, health or code compliance to the customer, including "flawless", "seamless" or gap-free joints. Reason: policy. Instead: refer questions to the owner and describe only the approved scope and finish level (source: master sections 1.4, 17 and 29; C-HI09-04).
9. Extending outlet or switch boxes, or moving, removing or rewiring electrical devices, in wainscot, baseboard or other trim zones. Reason: licensed trade. Instead: stop and escalate; any referral to a licensed electrician is an owner decision (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; spec START-011; 04 HI-09 permit/license flags).

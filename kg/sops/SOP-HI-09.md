## 1. Site preparation and property protection

### From "5. Customer and property preparation"

- PROPOSED checklist for review, sent with the appointment confirmation and reminder: move furniture and wall decor away from the work walls; plan access and pets; name items to protect; state any noise or dust sensitivity (source: 04 HI-09 "customer preparation requirements", PROPOSED; spec BRF-005, NTF-004, NTF-005). Who moves furniture is an owner decision; the agreement has no preparation clause (30 section 4.2, clause 5).
- Paint or stain responsibility is agreed before the visit; final paint coats are not part of this service unless a separate painting agreement covers them (source: spec HI-09.INT "paint/stain responsibility"; agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause).
- Every job re-confirms access, occupants, pets, parking, hazards and work-area conditions (source: spec CX-005).

  - Evidence: note

  - Evidence: note

If the work would remove, cut or sand existing painted trim or other painted surfaces in a home built before 1978 or of unknown build year, that work is held until a qualified person records a disposition (source: spec BRF-003 "approximate age if known"; 31 section 10 HI-09; X-13; Q-31-07).
  - Evidence: note

## 3. Service-specific work steps

### From "12. Step-by-step procedure" (introduction)

Original procedure text is kept verbatim and in its original order. Lines that start with HOLD or FLAG are added draft controls pending qualified review (source: 31 section 10 HI-09). A HOLD placed before a step must be cleared before that step starts; a HOLD or FLAG placed after a step limits or flags the step above it.

> HOLD (X-13): no step below removes, cuts or sands existing painted trim or other existing painted surfaces; removal of existing trim is not covered (section 2). If a job would require that in a home built before 1978 or of unknown build year, STOP before that work: it stays on hold until a qualified person records a disposition, and a customer statement does not clear it. Whether the EPA RRP rule applies is not concluded here (Q-31-07). Release: section 10. Pending review by an EPA RRP-certified renovator. (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; risk-triage playbook "A pre-1978 painted surface is not cleared by a customer saying it looks safe")

### From "12. Step-by-step procedure / 🚨 Problem Option A: Installing Crown Molding on Wavy Ceilings"

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Compound Cut Calculation" below, and before any other saw cut, the miter saw is used only with guarded tool use, eye and hearing protection and dust collection, as the spec card requires, and per the saw manufacturer's instructions. The guard, PPE and dust-collection selection is pending the qualified safety professional; until it is recorded, cutting stays on hold. Release: section 10. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection; dust collection"; HI-09.HAZ "saw/nailer injury, dust")

- [ ] **Compound Cut Calculation:** Set your dual-compound miter saw table angle and bevel tilt exactly to the wood's profile specification (standard is 31.6-degree miter and 33.9-degree bevel). Cut inside/outside corners upside-down and backward.

> FLAG: UNSOURCED - verify against manufacturer instructions. The settings "standard is 31.6-degree miter and 33.9-degree bevel" in the step above are kept verbatim and are not verified for any molding profile or corner. HYPOTHESIS for review: fixed compound settings suit only one molding spring angle and corner angle, and cutting corners "upside-down and backward" is usually an alternative to compound settings, not an addition to them. An experienced finish carpenter confirms the method and the settings from the molding profile, the measured corner geometry and the saw manufacturer's instructions. (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; 04 HI-09 "SOP reference"; spec HI-09.INT "corner geometry")

> HOLD (C-HI09-01; X-12): before "The Shimming Fix" below and the other ceiling-height steps of Option A, a ladder or other access equipment is used only under the approved height and access policy, which does not exist yet (02 G-005); until it does, ceiling-height work is held. No ladder type or setup rule is set here. Release: section 10. Pending qualified review. (source: spec HI-09.HAZ "ladder"; HI-09.SAF "ladder controls"; 31 section 8.4 rule 4, PROPOSED)

- [ ] **The Shimming Fix:** If the residential ceiling dips or waves, do not force the crown molding flat—it will warp the joint line. Slide thin wooden wedges (shims) behind the molding back edge to bridge hollow gaps.

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Pneumatic Brad Driving" below, scan every nailing path in the ceiling and wall framing for wiring, pipes and other hidden services with a scanner able to locate them, used per its manufacturer instructions, and record the result. If a service is detected or the result is inconclusive, STOP and escalate (section 19). Release: section 10. Pending qualified review. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "scan fastening paths"; HI-09.HAZ "hidden wiring/pipes")

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Pneumatic Brad Driving" below, the nailer is used only per its manufacturer's instructions, with guarded tool use and the eye and hearing protection that the qualified safety professional selects; until that selection is recorded, nailing stays on hold. Release: section 10. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection"; HI-09.HAZ "saw/nailer injury")

- [ ] **Pneumatic Brad Driving:** Fire 2-inch brad nails through the trim molding straight into solid wood ceiling plates and wall studs.

> FLAG (C-HI09-05): the step above names a pneumatic brad nailer, while the inventory lists a cordless brad nailer and no compressor or brad nails. Which fastening system is standard is an owner decision; use only the approved system and its manufacturer's fastener specification. (source: inventory file; spec HI-09.TLS "nailer/compressor or approved fastening system")

- [ ] **Finishing Touch:** Trim off protruding shims with a sharp utility knife, apply premium paintable latex caulk along the ceiling edge line to hide gaps, and fill brad holes with wood putty.

### From "12. Step-by-step procedure / 🚨 Problem Option B: Installing Wainscoting Box Panels on Flat Walls"

- [ ] **Grid Layout Blueprinting:** Snap vertical and horizontal chalk lines on the wall to grid out perfectly symmetrical wainscoting box patterns based on room dimensions.

> FLAG: if the grid in the step above reaches an outlet, switch or other electrical device, do not extend the box or move the device: STOP and escalate (Do not perform 9; section 10). Pending qualified review. (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; 04 HI-09 "stop-work conditions", PROPOSED; 02 G-054)

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Adhesive Layering" below, scan the pin-nailing paths along the chalk-line grid for wiring, pipes and other hidden services with a scanner able to locate them, used per its manufacturer instructions, and record the result; the panel pieces are pinned in "Fastener Setting" right after the adhesive is applied. If a service is detected or the result is inconclusive, STOP and escalate (section 19). Release: section 10. Pending qualified review. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "scan fastening paths"; HI-09.HAZ "hidden wiring/pipes")

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): the "pre-cut mitered trim pieces" named in "Adhesive Layering" below are cut only under the same saw controls as "Compound Cut Calculation" in Option A (guarded tool use, eye and hearing protection, dust collection), pending the qualified safety professional's selection. Release: section 10. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection; dust collection")

- [ ] **Adhesive Layering:** Apply heavy beads of liquid-nails construction adhesive to the back face of the pre-cut mitered trim pieces.

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Fastener Setting" below, the pin nailer is used only per its manufacturer's instructions, with guarded tool use and the eye and hearing protection that the qualified safety professional selects; until that selection is recorded, nailing stays on hold. Release: section 10. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection"; HI-09.HAZ "saw/nailer injury")

- [ ] **Fastener Setting:** Position the panel molding piece flat against your chalk line grid and secure it instantly using a pneumatic pin nailer. Caulk all inside joint boxes.

> FLAG (C-HI09-05; C-HI09-V01): the step above names a pneumatic pin nailer, while the inventory lists a cordless pin nailer and no pins; use only the approved fastening system (C-HI09-05). Whether wainscot attachment needs a fastener or framing rule in addition to the construction adhesive is an open question for an experienced finish carpenter; "secure attachment" is a quality check in section 13 (C-HI09-V01; spec HI-09.QAC). (source: inventory file; 02 C-HI09-V01)

## 6. Final checks, completion photos and walkthrough

### From "16. Photo requirements (before / during / after)"

- Before: arrival and existing-condition photos of the walls, ceilings and corners where trim goes, including dips and out-of-square conditions, and of nearby finishes (source: spec START-008, APXA-002; 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED).
  - Evidence: photo
- During: joints and corners before caulking and filling cover them (source: 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED; quality-assistant playbook).
- After: joint and corner close-ups, caulk and fill completion per room, a finish-readiness photo for the painting hand-off and the cleaned area, each tagged to its work area or task (source: spec QA-003; 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED).
- Photos are internal-only by default and avoid people, documents and valuables not needed for the work (source: spec REC-002; 31 section 3.5 rule 7).

### From "17. Customer communication"

- Arrival and completion updates are drafts; the worker or admin controls the send (source: spec UXE-008).
- Record the walkthrough outcome: accepted, accepted with punch items, unavailable or disputed (source: spec QA-004).
  - Evidence: note
- Painting hand-off: tell the customer that the trim is filled and caulked per the approved scope and ready for painting, and that final paint coats are not part of this service unless a separate painting agreement covers them (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause; 04 HI-09 exclusions).
- Care notes come only from approved product data, for example the caulk or adhesive label; none is written here (source: spec QA-008).
- Describe the method actually used: in Option A the crown is shimmed, brad-nailed into framing, caulked and filled; in Option B the panel molding is fixed with construction adhesive and pinned along a chalk-line grid. The website copy that says trim pieces are brad-nailed "onto framing lines" is flagged in the register (source: section 12; C-HI09-V01).
- Never promise "flawless", "seamless" or gap-free joints, and never repeat that wording from the main plan or the agreement; the finish standard is "tight joints appropriate to finish level" (source: Do not perform 8; C-HI09-04; spec HI-09.QAC).
- Blocked work: tell the customer only what the approved "work blocked" message says (source: spec NTF-007, COM-002).
- Price or scope changes, including baseboards, casings, sanding or trim removal, are not agreed on site without a recorded change order (source: spec JOB-005, FIN-008).

## Stop-work triggers, escalation and incidents (service-specific)

### From "10. Stop-work conditions"

The general stop-work rule is [[FACT-SVC-STOP-WORK-RULE]]. Service-specific triggers:

**HI-09 stop triggers (draft, pending qualified review).** In addition, stop the affected work and escalate (section 19) when:
- wiring, pipes or another hidden service is detected in a fastening path, or the scan is inconclusive (source: spec HI-09.HAZ "hidden wiring/pipes"; HI-09.SAF "scan fastening paths"; C-HI09-01);
- a saw or nailer guard is missing or damaged, a tool malfunctions, or the PPE and dust collection selected by the reviewer are not available (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection; dust collection"; JOB-008);
- the work needs a ladder or other access equipment and no approved height and access policy exists (source: spec HI-09.HAZ "ladder"; HI-09.SAF "ladder controls"; 02 G-005; X-12);
- the work would remove, cut or sand existing painted trim or other painted surfaces and the build year is before 1978 or unknown, or suspect material is found (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; X-13; spec START-011);
- an outlet or switch box would need extension, or an electrical device would need moving, to fit the trim or wainscot layout (source: 04 HI-09 "stop-work conditions", PROPOSED; Do not perform 9);
- the job requires baseboards, door or window casings, sanding or removal of existing trim that the approved scope does not include (source: section 2; spec JOB-010);
- the trim stock on site does not match the approved profile, material or quantity (source: spec START-006, JOB-004);
- occupants or pets cannot be kept clear of the work area (source: X-11).

Release role and release evidence for each trigger: `<reviewer decision: release role and evidence>` (31 section 8.3). Which of these triggers are critical holds that no role can override is pending (Q-31-18).

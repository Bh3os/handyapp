## 2. Scope

### In scope
- Room preparation and protection: moving lightweight furniture out, shifting and wrapping heavy furniture, floor protection, removing cover plates and masking trim (source: original Phase 1).
- Surface restoration: inspecting walls, filling nail holes, dents and cracks with spackle, sanding and spot-priming the repairs, subject to the older-coating HOLD before "Pole Sanding" (source: original Phase 2; section 12, C-PT01-01).
- Minor wall repair up to an owner-set size; larger repairs become DW-01 lines on the same estimate, with one minimum per bundle (source: [04](../AI%20Development%20Plans/04-service-registry.md) section 5.3 rule (a), PROPOSED; `<owner decision: patch-size threshold>`; Q-04-08).
- Cutting in along the ceiling boundary and the taped trim lines, and rolling the wall fields with the customer-approved product, color and sheen (source: original Phases 3 and 4; spec PT-01.INT "colors/finish, paint brand/product if specified").
- Tape removal, refitting the removed plates and covers, an inspection sweep with touch-up, and site restoration (source: original Phase 5).

### Out of scope
- Painting ceilings: Not covered - pending review (C-PT01-06). Pricing sells "Add Ceiling"; this SOP treats the ceiling only as a cut-line boundary.
- Painting trim, baseboards, window casings, doors and door frames: Not covered - pending review (C-PT01-06). This SOP masks them; pricing sells "Walls + Trim & Baseboards" and "Walls + Trim, Baseboards & Doors".
- Closet interiors: Not covered - pending review (C-PT01-06). Pricing sells "Closet Interiors".
- A full primer coat over the walls: Not covered - pending review (C-PT01-04). This SOP spot-primes raw spackle patches; the main plan promises a "high-hiding foundation primer" and the agreement's "Inclusions" clause names "priming". The owner decides: full coat, spot-prime only or a separate option (spec EST-005).
- Spray application: not covered; the spec card allows a sprayer only "if approved", and no approval exists (source: spec PT-01.TLS; 04 PT-01 "required tools").
- Wallpaper removal (PT-04), drywall repair above the patch-size threshold (DW-01), exterior surfaces, lead hazard work or abatement, mold or moisture remediation, and electrical or HVAC device work: no step covers them; the owner decides which become customer-visible exclusions (source: 04 PT-01 "exclusions").
- Moving heavy home-theater setups, pianos and fine art collections: the customer's task before arrival (source: agreement "Exclusions" clause, itself under attorney review).

## 10. Stop-work conditions

### 🕒 Phase 1: White-Glove Room Prep & Masking
- [ ] **Furniture Consolidation:** Move all lightweight furniture completely out of the room. Shift heavy furniture (sofas, large tables) to the exact center of the room.

> FLAG: the step above moves and shifts furniture with no weight limit, two-person rule or list of items not to move; the draft agreement leaves heavy home-theater setups, pianos and fine art collections to the customer (Do-not-perform entry 13). Limits pending owner and qualified safety review. (source: 04 PT-01 "hazards" and "exclusions"; agreement "Exclusions" clause)

- [ ] **Plastic Encapsulation:** Wrap the centered furniture cluster tightly in 1-mil plastic masking film to protect against floating paint overspray or dust.
- [ ] **Floor Defense:** Lay clean, heavy-weight canvas drop cloths across 100% of the exposed flooring. Secure edges with delicate surface tape to prevent sliding.
- [ ] **Hardware Removal:** Unscrew and remove all electrical switch plates, outlet covers, and wall-mounted thermostat faces. Put screws in a labeled zip bag.

> HOLD (C-PT01-V01): the step above is limited to removing covers: never touch wiring, devices or thermostat controls (Do-not-perform entry 10). Whether workers may remove wall-mounted thermostat faces at all is pending the owner and a licensed electrician or HVAC professional. If a face cannot be removed without touching wiring or controls, or a plate, device or thermostat is damaged or wiring is exposed, stop, photograph and escalate (spec JOB-004). Everything removed here is refitted before completion (HOLD after "Hardware Install"). Pending qualified review. (source: 31 section 10; 02 C-PT01-V01; 04 PT-01 "permit/license flags")

- [ ] **Trim Masking:** Apply premium painter's tape along the top edge of all baseboards, window casings, and door frames. Press down firmly with a plastic putty knife to seal the edge against paint bleed.

### 🩹 Phase 2: Surface Restoration & Priming

> HOLD (PT-01.SAF; X-11): before "Defect Patching" below, and before every later step that applies spackle, primer or paint, the reviewed SDS and the product label are on hand; ventilate the work area as the product instructions require; keep occupants, children and pets out of the work area, including a nursery or an allergy-sensitive room, until the label allows re-occupancy. No SDS, or ventilation not possible as the label requires: stop and escalate. Ventilation method TBD - qualified safety reviewer. Pending qualified review. (source: spec PT-01.SAF "ventilate", "follow product instructions"; spec PT-01.HAZ "VOC/chemical exposure"; 31 section 10; 29 section 8.3 interim rule, PROPOSED)

- [ ] **Defect Patching:** Inspect walls under high-intensity light. Fill nail holes, dents, and cracks using professional lightweight spackle.

> HOLD (C-PT01-01): before "Pole Sanding" below, check the recorded build year. If the home was built before 1978, or the build year is unknown, do not sand or scrape existing coatings (the whole-wall sanding in that step included) until a qualified person records the lead disposition; a customer saying the paint looks safe does not clear this hold. If an old texture or joint compound is suspected to contain asbestos, leave it undisturbed, stop and escalate. No abatement or lead-safe work method is given here. Pending review by an EPA RRP-certified renovator and an attorney licensed in KY/IN. (source: spec PT-01.SAF "assess older coatings before aggressive disturbance"; spec PT-01.HAZ "dust/lead in older coatings"; 31 section 10; risk-triage playbook "A pre-1978 painted surface is not cleared by a customer saying it looks safe."; X-13)

- [ ] **Pole Sanding:** Sand patched areas and the entire wall plane using a 120-grit pole sander to remove old paint textures and nubs. Wipe down walls with a dry microfiber tack cloth to capture powder.
- [ ] **Spot Priming:** Apply a high-hiding latex primer over raw spackle patches to seal them and prevent "flashing" (flat dull spots in the final coat).

> FLAG (C-PT01-04): the step above spot-primes raw spackle patches only. The main plan promises a full "foundation primer" coat and the agreement's "Inclusions" clause names "priming": full prime coat, spot-prime only or a separate option is an owner decision (spec EST-005). A full primer coat is not covered by this SOP (section 2). Pending owner review.

### 🪒 Phase 3: The Precision Edge Cut
- [ ] **Brush Loading:** Dip a clean 2.5-inch or 3-inch angled sash brush exactly 1 inch into the paint bucket. Tap the inner side of the bucket to remove excess (never wipe).

> HOLD (C-PT01-02): before "Ceiling and Trim Cut" below, and before any other work above floor reach, use a ladder or other access equipment only under an approved height and access policy, which does not exist yet (02 G-005). Ladder type, set-up, inspection and the maximum ceiling height served are TBD - qualified safety professional and owner (Q-31-09). Without approved access equipment on site, the affected task is blocked: record No Start or Blocked and escalate (spec SCH-008). Pending qualified review. (source: spec PT-01.SAF "ladder controls"; spec PT-01.HAZ "ladders"; 31 section 10; job-planner playbook "A missing ladder or unverified permit keeps affected tasks blocked.")

- [ ] **Ceiling and Trim Cut:** Execute a free-hand laser-straight cut-line along the ceiling boundary and taped trim lines. Maintain a continuous wet edge, working in 3-to-4 foot sections.

> FLAG (C-PT01-06): the step above cuts in along the ceiling boundary and the taped trim; painting the ceiling, trim, baseboards, doors or closet interiors is not covered by this SOP, although pricing sells them (section 2). Pending owner and lead-painter review.

- [ ] **Feathering the Cut:** Gently brush out the thick outer edge of your cut-line to prevent a raised paint ridge from showing later.

### 🌀 Phase 4: Rolling the Open Fields
- [ ] **Roller Load:** Saturate a 3/8-inch or 1/2-inch lint-free microfiber roller nap uniformly in the paint tray grid.
- [ ] **The "W" Pattern:** Apply paint to the wall in a large, sweeping "W" or "N" pattern (approx. 3x3 feet) to distribute the bulk load.
- [ ] **Fill & Back-Roll:** Roll back across the "W" in vertical, overlapping ceiling-to-floor strokes to smooth out shadows. *Crucial:* Always maintain a wet edge and finish rolling an entire wall panel before stopping to prevent visible seam lines.

> FLAG (C-PT01-04): no step states the number of coats or the recoat interval. Coats follow the approved estimate (the agreement's "Inclusions" clause names a minimum number of coats); recoat and dry times follow the approved product label; no interval is set here (section 8). Pending owner review.

### ✨ Phase 5: Reassembly, Clean-up & Quality Audit
- [ ] **Tape Pull:** Pull all painters tape slowly at a sharp 45-degree angle while the second coat of paint is still slightly damp to avoid tearing dry paint edges.
- [ ] **Hardware Install:** Re-screw all clean electrical plates and outlet covers back onto the walls once dry.

> HOLD (C-PT01-V01): the step above refits electrical plates and outlet covers only. Before completion, every item removed in "Hardware Removal", including any thermostat face, is refitted as found and the result is recorded (section 13). If an item cannot be refitted without touching wiring or controls, stop and escalate; no electrical or HVAC procedure is given here. Pending review by a qualified safety professional and a licensed electrician or HVAC professional. (source: spec APXA-005 "restore utilities/components altered within scope"; 31 section 10; 02 C-PT01-V01)

- [ ] **The Flashlight Sweep:** Run an LED inspection beam flat against the wall profile to double-check for roller skips, holidays, or sagging drips. Touch up immediately.
- [ ] **Site Restoration:** Gather canvas cloths, vacuum up minor debris, and move furniture back to its original layout.

## 19. Escalation
> 1. Stop the affected work and keep yourself, occupants and pets away from the hazard; do not try to fix, move or disturb it (source: spec START-011; risk-triage playbook L7).
> 2. Emergency (fire, gas odor, injury, electrical contact, structural movement): call emergency services first, then `<owner decision: emergency contact>` (source: spec BRF-013).
> 3. Only if policy allows and it is safe, take the minimum action needed to prevent immediate injury or property damage, and record why and what you did (source: spec JOB-006).
> 4. Report in the app with Stop Work / Report Hazard: type, photos and severity (source: spec JOB-004, UXE-004). Before the job has started, record No Start / Blocked instead of starting (source: spec SCH-008, AT-030).
> 5. Escalate with one tap to `<owner decision: escalation order>`; the item enters the owner/supervisor queue (source: spec UXE-002, JOB-012).
> 6. Wait for release by the role named in the hold, with evidence recorded. Critical holds clear only with a recorded qualified assessment (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; 27 Roles and permissions section 6.3).
> 7. Tell the customer only what the approved "work blocked" message says; never send incident details or access codes by text or email (source: spec NTF-007, COM-002).
> 8. Resume only after the briefing is updated and re-acknowledged, with a change order if scope or price changes (source: spec START-002, FIN-008, JOB-005).

PT-01 routes (contacts are owner decisions, Q-31-05):

- A customer request for ceilings, trim, doors, closets or a full primer coat that is not on the approved estimate: record it; change order (C-PT01-06, C-PT01-04).

## 20. Incident reporting
> - Report every injury, property damage, near miss, customer conflict, tool or equipment failure and suspected hazardous-material exposure on the incident form (source: spec JOB-008).
> - An injured person comes first: call emergency services; give only the first aid that the company's approved first-response arrangements cover (not yet defined; pending qualified safety review); medical decisions belong to emergency services and medical professionals (source: master section 1.3; safety-liability-manager skill "prioritize immediate safety and emergency response over paperwork").
> - Record facts, photos and actions taken. Do not guess causes or discuss fault or liability with the customer; refer those questions to the owner (source: spec AIR-012 boundary "liability admissions require human review").
> - Keep injury and medical details out of customer-visible notes and messages (source: spec PERM-003, COM-002).
> - Report as soon as it is safe and before the job is closed (source: spec Appendix A APXA-007). High-severity incidents go to the escalation queue (source: spec AT-035, JOB-012).

- PT-01 examples: a fall from a ladder or step, a paint or chemical spill on customer property, damage to furniture, floors or fixtures, suspected lead-dust or chemical exposure, and contact with electrical wiring (source: spec JOB-008; spec PT-01.HAZ).

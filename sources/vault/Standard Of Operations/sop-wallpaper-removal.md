## 1. Purpose

A damage-free, patient chemical and steam operational sequence designed to lift vintage paper layers while preserving fragile drywall framing underneath.

> FLAG: outcome claim - owner and attorney review (C-PT04-05). "damage-free" in the original statement above conflicts with the agreement's "Damage Disclaimer" ("minor paper tearing of the sheet core may occur during removal"), with this SOP's own "Torn Paper Seal" step and with the pricing "Unprimed Drywall Fine" line. The statement stays verbatim until a reviewed SOP version replaces it (31 section 3.5 rule 5; Q-31-16). The Phase 3 heading in section 12 repeats "Damage-Free".

- Remove wallpaper and its adhesive after a test of the removal method, wash off the residue, and skim-repair minor damage to an agreed finish level ready for primer, for PT-04 Wallpaper Removal & Surface Restoration (source: spec Appendix B; spec PT-04.SAF, PT-04.QAC; 04 PT-04 "customer-facing description", PROPOSED neutral text).
- Primer and paint coats are applied under separate agreements, such as PT-01 (source: agreement "Payment Due" clause; 04 PT-04 "customer-facing description"). The spot seal of torn paper in "Torn Paper Seal" stays in this SOP; whether any primer coat is included is an owner decision (source: spec PT-04.QAC "primed if included"; 04 PT-04 "quality checklist").
- This SOP makes no outcome, finish, safety or health promise to the customer (source: 31 section 3.5 rule 5).

## 2. Scope

### In scope
- Floor and baseboard protection with plastic sheeting (source: original "Floor Protection").
- A removal-method test, then scoring only where appropriate, softening with enzyme solution, steam where the glue is stubborn, and lifting the paper with rounded scrapers (source: original Phases 2 and 3; spec PT-04.TLS "scorer only when appropriate"; spec PT-04.SAF "test removal method").
- Removal of residual adhesive and a fresh-water wash (source: original Phase 4).
- Sealing minor tears in the drywall paper and spot skim coating of rough spots, indents and seams, then sanding (source: original Phase 5; pricing "Inclusions" line "basic Level 4 skim coat mud touch-up"; agreement "Inclusions" clause).
- Electrical isolation at outlets in the work area: the original steps are held pending licensed-electrician review (section 12; C-PT04-03).

### Out of scope
- A "flawless, paint-ready Level 4 finish" over whole walls, as the main plan describes: Not covered - pending review (C-PT04-06). This SOP skims "rough spots, indents, and seams" only; the deliverable and its acceptance criteria are an owner decision (X-26).
- Drywall reconstruction after hidden mold (agreement "Inclusions" clause, "paid change order"): Not covered - pending review (C-PT04-02). Suspected mold stops the work (section 10); assessment, remediation and any reconstruction need a qualified mold professional and an owner decision on referral.
- Repair of tearing beyond the minor tears that "Torn Paper Seal" covers, including the "extensive wide trowel patching" of the pricing "Unprimed Drywall Fine" line: Not covered - pending review (C-PT04-05). The threshold between minor and wide tearing is undefined; any added repair goes through a change order that the customer approves before the work (spec FIN-008, JOB-005).
- Adhesive that the enzyme solution and steam do not loosen (the pricing "Hourly Safe-Fallback Rate" line names "completely crystallized or non-water-soluble" glue): Not covered - pending review (C-PT04-07). No other product or method is added here; another method needs its product instructions and review.
- Border removal along the ceiling (pricing "Border-Only Stripping Surcharge") and any other work beyond floor-standing reach: ladder work only under an approved height and access policy, which does not exist yet (X-12; 02 G-005).
- Plaster walls, ceiling paper, wallpaper hanging, fixture removal, furniture moving and texture matching: no step covers them; the owner decides which become customer-visible exclusions (source: 04 PT-04 "exclusions", GAP).

Pending review by an EPA RRP-certified renovator and an accredited asbestos inspector. (source: spec PT-04.SAF "stop on suspect hazardous coating/material"; spec PT-04.INT "age, painted/unpainted wall"; 02 C-PT04-04; X-13; risk-triage playbook "A pre-1978 painted surface is not cleared by a customer saying it looks safe.")

> HOLD (C-PT04-07): before "Wall Scoring" below, run a test patch of the removal method and record the method decision (whether to score, how to apply the solution, whether steam is needed), with a photo. Pending owner and lead-technician review with the product instructions. (source: spec PT-04.SAF "test removal method"; spec PT-04.TLS "scorer only when appropriate"; 02 C-PT04-07 "test patch + method decision recorded")

- [ ] **Wall Scoring:** Use an ergonomic wallpaper scoring tool (like a Paper Tiger) to roll over the wallpaper using light pressure. Make circular patterns to puncture the outer vinyl layer *without piercing the drywall paper below*.

> FLAG (C-PT04-07): the step above is conditional: score only when appropriate, as the removal-method test decides; the card does not make scoring a default. Pending owner and lead-technician review. (source: spec PT-04.TLS "scorer only when appropriate"; 02 C-PT04-07)

- [ ] **Enzyme Mix:** Mix a warm water batch of professional enzyme-based wallpaper stripper (e.g., DIF brand) inside a clean garden pump sprayer.
- [ ] **Saturation Pass:** Spray the chemical solution generously over a 4x6 foot wall section. Allow the formula to absorb for 15 minutes to liquefy the old starched wheat paste beneath.

> FLAG (C-PT04-07): "generously" in the step above is in tension with the card's "avoid excessive saturation". Apply the solution per the product label, as the removal-method test decides; water or solution reaching an electrical box is a stop condition (section 10). The dwell time in the step above stays unchanged pending a label check. The same applies to the second spray in "Glue Stripping". Pending owner and lead-technician review. (source: spec PT-04.SAF "avoid excessive saturation"; 02 C-PT04-07 "per product label; avoid excessive saturation (spec)", "Dwell times unchanged pending label check")

### 💨 Phase 3: Steam Extraction & Damage-Free Scraping
- [ ] **Steam Plate Apply:** If the glue is stubborn, hold a commercial wallpaper steam plate flat against the wet paper for 15-20 seconds to force moisture deep into the adhesive backing.
- [ ] **The Gentle Slide:** Use a wide, flexible, rounded-edge putty knife. Slide the blade under the softened paper seams at a shallow 15-degree angle. Fluidly lift the sheets away in large panels. *Never force or gouge the drywall.*

> HOLD (C-PT04-02): if suspected mold or moisture is found behind the paper lifted in the step above, STOP work on that wall, keep people and pets away, photograph it and escalate (JOB-004). Do not clean, scrape, remove or rebuild the affected material; the agreement's "paid change order" for reconstruction does not replace this stop. Pending review by a mold assessor and the qualified safety professional. (source: spec START-011 "suspect hazardous materials"; spec JOB-004; 02 C-PT04-02; X-06; 31 section 8.5 escalation step 1; Q-31-10)

### 🧼 Phase 4: Residual Glue Scraping & Wash
- [ ] **Glue Stripping:** Spray the bare walls a second time with warm enzyme solution.
- [ ] **Scrubbing Grime:** Use a heavy-duty coarse abrasive sponge to scrub the walls down, pulling away sticky paste ribbons.
- [ ] **Fresh Water Flush:** Wipe down the bare walls using clean sponges and a bucket of pure warm water to remove all chemical residue. Let walls dry completely for 24 hours.

### ✨ Phase 5: Skim Coating & Final Sanding
- [ ] **Torn Paper Seal:** If any minor brown drywall paper tore during removal, seal it immediately with an oil-based primer to prevent compound bubbling later.
- [ ] **Skim Coat Finish:** Apply a thin layer of lightweight joint compound (معجون) over rough spots, indents, and seams using a 10-inch or 12-inch taping knife.

> FLAG (C-PT04-11): the step above contains an Arabic word, kept verbatim as a legacy artifact; it is not a translation. Worker-facing text is English, with Spanish only through the reviewed translation workflow. Pending the owner's cleanup decision with the bilingual reviewer. (source: spec BIZ-002; owner facts 2026-10-02; 36 section 5.4 item 21)

- [ ] **Dustless Polish:** Sand the dry compound using 220-grit sponges or a HEPA dustless sander to leave a smooth Level 4 finish ready for interior primer.

> FLAG (C-PT04-04): "or a HEPA dustless sander" in the step above leaves dust capture optional; the dust-control method is pending qualified safety review, and the review may only strengthen it. The older-coating hold before "Wall Scoring" applies to sanding as well. (source: spec PT-04.SAF "control dust"; spec PT-04.TLS "sanding/HEPA vacuum"; 02 C-PT04-04)

> FLAG (X-26; C-PT04-06): "smooth Level 4 finish" in the step above has no shared definition or acceptance criteria yet, and the main plan's "flawless, paint-ready Level 4 finish" is not covered (section 2). Pending the owner's decision with an experienced drywall finisher. (source: 02 X-26, C-PT04-06)

> HOLD (C-PT04-03): the procedure above ends without restoring what Phase 1 altered. Before completion, anything placed in or over electrical boxes is removed and any circuit switched off is restored, by the method the licensed electrician approves; no restore procedure is written here. Pending licensed-electrician review. (source: spec APXA-005 "restore utilities/components altered within scope"; 02 C-PT04-03; X-07)

## 19. Escalation
> 1. Stop the affected work and keep yourself, occupants and pets away from the hazard; do not try to fix, move or disturb it (source: spec START-011; risk-triage playbook L7).
> 2. Emergency (fire, gas odor, injury, electrical contact, structural movement): call emergency services first, then `<owner decision: emergency contact>` (source: spec BRF-013).
> 3. Only if policy allows and it is safe, take the minimum action needed to prevent immediate injury or property damage, and record why and what you did (source: spec JOB-006).
> 4. Report in the app with Stop Work / Report Hazard: type, photos and severity (source: spec JOB-004, UXE-004). Before the job has started, record No Start / Blocked instead of starting (source: spec SCH-008, AT-030).
> 5. Escalate with one tap to `<owner decision: escalation order>`; the item enters the owner/supervisor queue (source: spec UXE-002, JOB-012).
> 6. Wait for release by the role named in the hold, with evidence recorded. Critical holds clear only with a recorded qualified assessment (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; 27 Roles and permissions section 6.3).
> 7. Tell the customer only what the approved "work blocked" message says; never send incident details or access codes by text or email (source: spec NTF-007, COM-002).
> 8. Resume only after the briefing is updated and re-acknowledged, with a change order if scope or price changes (source: spec START-002, FIN-008, JOB-005).

PT-04 routes (contacts are owner decisions, Q-31-05):
- Electrical: the isolation cannot be verified, water reaches a box, or wiring is exposed or damaged: stop; the office refers it to a licensed electrician (02 C-PT04-03).
- Suspect older coating or compound: stop; the owner arranges assessment by an EPA RRP-certified renovator or an accredited asbestos inspector, per the owner's policy (02 C-PT04-04; Q-31-07; Q-30-18).
- Suspected mold or moisture: stop; assessment by a mold assessor; the referral path is an owner decision (02 C-PT04-02; Q-31-10).
- Tearing beyond minor tears, or a different removal method needed: stop that task; the owner or the estimator prepares a change order before any added work (spec FIN-008, JOB-005).

## 20. Incident reporting
> - Report every injury, property damage, near miss, customer conflict, tool or equipment failure and suspected hazardous-material exposure on the incident form (source: spec JOB-008).
> - An injured person comes first: call emergency services; give only the first aid that the company's approved first-response arrangements cover (not yet defined; pending qualified safety review); medical decisions belong to emergency services and medical professionals (source: master section 1.3; safety-liability-manager skill "prioritize immediate safety and emergency response over paperwork").
> - Record facts, photos and actions taken. Do not guess causes or discuss fault or liability with the customer; refer those questions to the owner (source: spec AIR-012 boundary "liability admissions require human review").
> - Keep injury and medical details out of customer-visible notes and messages (source: spec PERM-003, COM-002).
> - Report as soon as it is safe and before the job is closed (source: spec Appendix A APXA-007). High-severity incidents go to the escalation queue (source: spec AT-035, JOB-012).

- PT-04 examples: a steam or hot-water burn, an electrical shock or short, a chemical splash or exposure, a slip on wet sheeting, water damage to floors or other property, and a ladder incident (source: spec JOB-008; spec PT-04.HAZ).

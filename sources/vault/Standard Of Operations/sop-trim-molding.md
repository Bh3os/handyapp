## 1. Purpose

Install new decorative trim: crown molding where the ceiling dips or waves (Option A) and wainscoting box panels on flat walls (Option B), cut, fitted and fastened so that joints are tight appropriate to the finish level, pieces are securely attached, reveals and alignment are consistent, holes and seams are filled or caulked per the approved scope, and the surface is ready for the agreed finish (source: spec Appendix B HI-09; spec HI-09.QAC; original Option A and B headings in section 12). Painting is not part of this service unless a separate painting agreement covers it (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause). This draft authorizes no work and promises no result to the customer (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; 31 section 3.5 rule 5).

## 2. Scope

### In scope
- Option A: crown molding on ceilings that dip or wave: compound miter cutting, shimming, brad nailing into wood framing, then trimming the shims, caulking the ceiling edge and filling brad holes (source: section 12, Option A).
- Option B: wainscoting box panels on flat walls: chalk-line grid layout, construction adhesive, pin nailing and caulking of inside joints (source: section 12, Option B).
- Paint preparation limited to the agreement's inclusions: "mechanical installation, brad counter-sinking, wood putty filling, and continuous edge caulking" (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause).

### Out of scope
- Not covered - pending review (C-HI09-02): baseboards. The agreement's "Scope Details" name baseboards and the pricing file prices "Standard Baseboards & Door/Window Casings", but no step installs baseboards (source: agreement "Scope Details" clause; pricing file; 31 section 10 HI-09).
- Not covered - pending review (C-HI09-02): door and window casings. They are priced in the pricing file and named in the main plan ("window casings"), but no step installs them and the agreement scope does not name them (source: pricing file; main plan section 9; agreement "Scope Details" clause).
- Not covered - pending review (C-HI09-06): sanding. The main plan promises to "sand joints smooth", but neither the agreement's inclusions nor any step includes sanding, which creates dust and, on older painted surfaces, a lead-paint concern (source: main plan section 9; agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause; 02 C-HI09-06).
- Not covered - pending owner decision: removal and disposal of existing trim; wall or ceiling repair beyond caulking; stain-grade or clear finishing; profile sizes and multi-piece crown that the pricing file does not price; supply of the trim stock (source: 04 HI-09 exclusions and pricing model; 02 G-054; Q-29-06).
- Not covered: final paint coats, unless a separate painting agreement covers them; trim painting is a PT-01 line (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause; 04 HI-09 exclusions).

### Do not perform
1. Electrical, plumbing, gas or HVAC work outside the approved scope or requiring a licensed trade. Reason: licensed trade. Instead: stop and escalate (source: spec START-011 "unapproved electrical/plumbing/gas/structural scope"; master section 1.3).
2. Structural work, meaning cutting, removing, sistering or adding load-bearing members, ledgers, footings or framing, unless it is in the approved scope with the required qualified review. Reason: structural. Instead: stop and escalate (source: spec START-011; master section 1.3).
3. Disturbing suspect asbestos, lead paint or mold. This includes removing, cutting or sanding existing painted trim or other painted surfaces in a home built before 1978 or of unknown build year until a qualified person records a disposition; whether the EPA RRP rule applies is not concluded here (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; X-13; risk-triage playbook "A pre-1978 painted surface is not cleared by a customer saying it looks safe"; owner facts 2026-10-02 "Unknown is not safe"). Reason: hazardous material. Suspected asbestos / lead paint / mold: stop and escalate (source: spec START-011).
4. Work that is not in the approved scope or an approved change order, including baseboards, casings, sanding or removal of existing trim until the owner approves them (section 2). Reason: out of scope. Instead: record the customer's request for a change order (source: spec JOB-005, JOB-010, FIN-008, AT-033).
5. Working at height or using access equipment beyond the approved height and access policy. No such policy exists yet (02 G-005), so crown work and any other work that needs a ladder is held until it does (31 section 8.4 rule 4, PROPOSED). Reason: policy. Instead: stop and escalate (source: HI-06.SAF "beyond approved height/access policy"; DW-04.TLS "scaffolds/stilts only under approved policy"; spec HI-09.HAZ "ladder"; HI-09.SAF "ladder controls").
6. Mixing or sequencing chemicals in any way the approved step and the product SDS do not allow. Reason: hazardous material. Instead: stop and escalate (source: CL-01.SAF "never mix incompatible chemicals"; X-09).
7. Recording, photographing or sending customer passwords, master codes or site access codes outside the app's secured fields. Reason: policy (privacy). Instead: use the app's secured fields only (source: spec COM-002; owner facts 2026-10-02; 27 Roles and permissions AUTH-009).
8. Promising results, warranties, safety, health or code compliance to the customer, including "flawless", "seamless" or gap-free joints. Reason: policy. Instead: refer questions to the owner and describe only the approved scope and finish level (source: master sections 1.4, 17 and 29; C-HI09-04).
9. Extending outlet or switch boxes, or moving, removing or rewiring electrical devices, in wainscot, baseboard or other trim zones. Reason: licensed trade. Instead: stop and escalate; any referral to a licensed electrician is an owner decision (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; spec START-011; 04 HI-09 permit/license flags).

## 10. Stop-work conditions

> **Stop-work rule (all HandyHub services).** You have the right to stop work. For any condition below, stop the affected work and escalate before continuing (source: spec START-011, JOB-004):
> - an unsafe condition for you, the customer, occupants, pets or the public;
> - concealed damage, substrate failure, moisture or an active leak;
> - suspect hazardous material, such as possible asbestos, lead paint or mold; do not disturb it;
> - electrical, plumbing, gas, HVAC or structural work that is not in the approved scope or needs a licensed trade;
> - an aggressive occupant or animal;
> - severe weather or unsafe heat or cold (source: spec BRF-008);
> - any material mismatch between the site and the briefing, approved scope, materials, access or approvals.
>
> Do not continue through an unsafe or restricted condition to keep the schedule (source: master section 12). A customer request, a checkbox or a signature does not clear a safety hold (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; risk-triage playbook L27). In an emergency, move people away and call emergency services first (source: spec BRF-013; risk-triage playbook L7). Then use Stop Work / Report Hazard in the app (source: spec UXE-004) and follow section 19.

### 🚨 Problem Option A: Installing Crown Molding on Wavy Ceilings

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Compound Cut Calculation" below, and before any other saw cut, the miter saw is used only with guarded tool use, eye and hearing protection and dust collection, as the spec card requires, and per the saw manufacturer's instructions. The guard, PPE and dust-collection selection is pending the qualified safety professional; until it is recorded, cutting stays on hold. Release: section 10. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection; dust collection"; HI-09.HAZ "saw/nailer injury, dust")

- [ ] **Compound Cut Calculation:** Set your dual-compound miter saw table angle and bevel tilt exactly to the wood's profile specification (standard is 31.6-degree miter and 33.9-degree bevel). Cut inside/outside corners upside-down and backward.

> FLAG: UNSOURCED - verify against manufacturer instructions. The settings "standard is 31.6-degree miter and 33.9-degree bevel" in the step above are kept verbatim and are not verified for any molding profile or corner. HYPOTHESIS for review: fixed compound settings suit only one molding spring angle and corner angle, and cutting corners "upside-down and backward" is usually an alternative to compound settings, not an addition to them. An experienced finish carpenter confirms the method and the settings from the molding profile, the measured corner geometry and the saw manufacturer's instructions. (source: 31 section 10 HI-09; 04 HI-09 "SOP reference"; spec HI-09.INT "corner geometry")

> HOLD (C-HI09-01; X-12): before "The Shimming Fix" below and the other ceiling-height steps of Option A, a ladder or other access equipment is used only under the approved height and access policy, which does not exist yet (02 G-005); until it does, ceiling-height work is held. No ladder type or setup rule is set here. Release: section 10. Pending qualified review. (source: spec HI-09.HAZ "ladder"; HI-09.SAF "ladder controls"; 31 section 8.4 rule 4, PROPOSED)

- [ ] **The Shimming Fix:** If the residential ceiling dips or waves, do not force the crown molding flat—it will warp the joint line. Slide thin wooden wedges (shims) behind the molding back edge to bridge hollow gaps.

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Pneumatic Brad Driving" below, scan every nailing path in the ceiling and wall framing for wiring, pipes and other hidden services with a scanner able to locate them, used per its manufacturer instructions, and record the result. If a service is detected or the result is inconclusive, STOP and escalate (section 19). Release: section 10. Pending qualified review. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "scan fastening paths"; HI-09.HAZ "hidden wiring/pipes")

> HOLD (C-HI09-01): before "Pneumatic Brad Driving" below, the nailer is used only per its manufacturer's instructions, with guarded tool use and the eye and hearing protection that the qualified safety professional selects; until that selection is recorded, nailing stays on hold. Release: section 10. (source: spec HI-09.SAF "guarded tool use; eye/hearing protection"; HI-09.HAZ "saw/nailer injury")

- [ ] **Pneumatic Brad Driving:** Fire 2-inch brad nails through the trim molding straight into solid wood ceiling plates and wall studs.

> FLAG (C-HI09-05): the step above names a pneumatic brad nailer, while the inventory lists a cordless brad nailer and no compressor or brad nails. Which fastening system is standard is an owner decision; use only the approved system and its manufacturer's fastener specification. (source: inventory file; spec HI-09.TLS "nailer/compressor or approved fastening system")

- [ ] **Finishing Touch:** Trim off protruding shims with a sharp utility knife, apply premium paintable latex caulk along the ceiling edge line to hide gaps, and fill brad holes with wood putty.

## 14. Cleanup
- Collect trim offcuts and shim trimmings, and vacuum sawdust from the work area (source: spec QA-002; HI-09.SAF "dust collection").
- Remove protection, account for tools and materials, and restore components altered within scope (source: spec APXA-005).
- Return unused materials and record leftover stock (source: spec APXA-007).

## 15. Waste handling
- Expected waste from the current steps: trim offcuts, shim trimmings, sawdust, empty caulk and adhesive tubes and packaging (source: section 12; inventory file).
- Destination, who removes the waste and any charge: TBD — owner decision; no waste policy exists (source: 31 section 3.1; 02 G-078).
- Empty or partly used chemical containers are handled per the product SDS once the reviewer has summarized it (source: 29 section 8.2, control 10).
- Removal or disposal of existing trim is not covered (section 2).

## 16. Photo requirements (before / during / after)
- Before: arrival and existing-condition photos of the walls, ceilings and corners where trim goes, including dips and out-of-square conditions, and of nearby finishes (source: spec START-008, APXA-002; 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED).
- During: joints and corners before caulking and filling cover them (source: 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED; quality-assistant playbook).
- After: joint and corner close-ups, caulk and fill completion per room, a finish-readiness photo for the painting hand-off and the cleaned area, each tagged to its work area or task (source: spec QA-003; 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED).
- Photos are internal-only by default and avoid people, documents and valuables not needed for the work (source: spec REC-002; 31 section 3.5 rule 7).

## 17. Customer communication
- Arrival and completion updates are drafts; the worker or admin controls the send (source: spec UXE-008).
- Record the walkthrough outcome: accepted, accepted with punch items, unavailable or disputed (source: spec QA-004).
- Painting hand-off: tell the customer that the trim is filled and caulked per the approved scope and ready for painting, and that final paint coats are not part of this service unless a separate painting agreement covers them (source: agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause; 04 HI-09 exclusions).
- Care notes come only from approved product data, for example the caulk or adhesive label; none is written here (source: spec QA-008).
- Describe the method actually used: in Option A the crown is shimmed, brad-nailed into framing, caulked and filled; in Option B the panel molding is fixed with construction adhesive and pinned along a chalk-line grid. The website copy that says trim pieces are brad-nailed "onto framing lines" is flagged in the register (source: section 12; C-HI09-V01).
- Never promise "flawless", "seamless" or gap-free joints, and never repeat that wording from the main plan or the agreement; the finish standard is "tight joints appropriate to finish level" (source: Do not perform 8; C-HI09-04; spec HI-09.QAC).
- Blocked work: tell the customer only what the approved "work blocked" message says (source: spec NTF-007, COM-002).
- Price or scope changes, including baseboards, casings, sanding or trim removal, are not agreed on site without a recorded change order (source: spec JOB-005, FIN-008).

## 18. Completion criteria
- All section 13 checks answered or an exception recorded (source: spec QA-001).
- After photos captured and mapped to tasks (source: spec QA-003).
- Cleanup done, protection removed, tools and materials accounted for (source: spec QA-002, APXA-005).
- Scan results recorded for every fastening path (source: spec HI-09.SAF; C-HI09-01).
- Painting hand-off note recorded where painting follows, stating that the trim is filled and caulked per scope and that paint coats are not part of this service (source: 04 HI-09 "completion evidence", PROPOSED; agreement "Prep vs Painting" clause).
- Walkthrough outcome recorded (source: spec QA-004).
- Unresolved issues, incidents and change follow-ups submitted before the job is closed (source: spec APXA-007).
- The worker recommends completion for human sign-off; a customer signature does not clear an unresolved safety hold (source: quality-assistant playbook).

## 19. Escalation

> 1. Stop the affected work and keep yourself, occupants and pets away from the hazard; do not try to fix, move or disturb it (source: spec START-011; risk-triage playbook L7).
> 2. Emergency (fire, gas odor, injury, electrical contact, structural movement): call emergency services first, then `<owner decision: emergency contact>` (source: spec BRF-013).
> 3. Only if policy allows and it is safe, take the minimum action needed to prevent immediate injury or property damage, and record why and what you did (source: spec JOB-006).
> 4. Report in the app with Stop Work / Report Hazard: type, photos and severity (source: spec JOB-004, UXE-004). Before the job has started, record No Start / Blocked instead of starting (source: spec SCH-008, AT-030).
> 5. Escalate with one tap to `<owner decision: escalation order>`; the item enters the owner/supervisor queue (source: spec UXE-002, JOB-012).
> 6. Wait for release by the role named in the hold, with evidence recorded. Critical holds clear only with a recorded qualified assessment (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; 27 Roles and permissions section 6.3).
> 7. Tell the customer only what the approved "work blocked" message says; never send incident details or access codes by text or email (source: spec NTF-007, COM-002).
> 8. Resume only after the briefing is updated and re-acknowledged, with a change order if scope or price changes (source: spec START-002, FIN-008, JOB-005).

- HI-09 roles and contacts: `<owner decision: escalation order and contacts>` (Q-31-05).

## 20. Incident reporting

> - Report every injury, property damage, near miss, customer conflict, tool or equipment failure and suspected hazardous-material exposure on the incident form (source: spec JOB-008).
> - An injured person comes first: call emergency services; give only the first aid that the company's approved first-response arrangements cover (not yet defined; pending qualified safety review); medical decisions belong to emergency services and medical professionals (source: master section 1.3; safety-liability-manager skill "prioritize immediate safety and emergency response over paperwork").
> - Record facts, photos and actions taken. Do not guess causes or discuss fault or liability with the customer; refer those questions to the owner (source: spec AIR-012 boundary "liability admissions require human review").
> - Keep injury and medical details out of customer-visible notes and messages (source: spec PERM-003, COM-002).
> - Report as soon as it is safe and before the job is closed (source: spec Appendix A APXA-007). High-severity incidents go to the escalation queue (source: spec AT-035, JOB-012).

## 1. Purpose

Recurring hotel-style housekeeping of a private residence for a resident who subscribes to a plan of visits: garment care within care labels, bed and linen presentation, light tidying and dusting, room scenting where the customer wants it, waste and floor care, and a secure closeout, done with strict privacy and without disturbing high-value items except for authorized care, for the canonical service CL-06 Hotel-Style Luxury Housekeeping (source: spec Appendix B; spec CL-06.INT, CL-06.SAF, CL-06.QAC; 04 CL-06 "customer-facing description", PROPOSED basis for a rewrite). No outcome is promised (source: 31 section 3.5 rule 5).

- The original file has no introduction line; its procedure is in section 12, unchanged. Its title calls it a "Micro-Checklist" and drops "Luxury" from the canonical name, which the frontmatter carries (source: 04 CL-06 "aliases").
- The agreement's scope sentence refers to a `hotel-housekeeping.md` micro-checklist, which does not exist; this file is the CL-06 SOP. The agreement reference changes only through attorney review (C-CL06-09; 30 section 6). This file keeps its name until the owner approves the rename to `sop-hotel-housekeeping.md` (34 step 2.10).

## 2. Scope

### In scope

## 10. Stop-work conditions

> **Stop-work rule (all HandyHub services).** You have the right to stop work. For any condition below, stop the affected work and escalate before continuing (source: spec START-011, JOB-004):
> - an unsafe condition for you, the customer, occupants, pets or the public;
> - concealed damage, substrate failure, moisture or an active leak;
> - suspect hazardous material, such as possible asbestos, lead paint or mold; do not disturb it;
> - electrical, plumbing, gas, HVAC or structural work that is not in the approved scope or needs a licensed trade;
> - an aggressive occupant or animal;
> - severe weather or unsafe heat or cold (source: spec BRF-008);
> - any material mismatch between the site and the briefing, approved scope, materials, access or approvals.
>
> Do not continue through an unsafe or restricted condition to keep the schedule (source: master section 12). A customer request, a checkbox or a signature does not clear a safety hold (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; risk-triage playbook L27). In an emergency, move people away and call emergency services first (source: spec BRF-013; risk-triage playbook L7). Then use Stop Work / Report Hazard in the app (source: spec UXE-004) and follow section 19.

Service-specific stop triggers (source: spec CL-06.SAF unless noted; PROPOSED wording pending qualified review):
- A task would need opening, reading or photographing mail, documents or other personal records: do not; escalate (CL-06.SAF "strict privacy; do not photograph/access personal records without need").
- A garment has no clear care label, or its label does not allow the planned pressing; a surface's finish or care instructions are unknown: do not treat that item; set it aside and report it (CL-06.SAF "follow garment/surface care labels").
- Valuables found out of place or missing, or damage found or caused: stop handling the item and escalate (CL-06.SAF "escalation for valuables/damage"; spec JOB-008 for damage).
- A fragrance allergy or sensitivity is reported, or the fragrance check is not on record: no scent products (02 C-CL06-02).
- No reviewed SDS for a product: do not use it (29 section 8.3).
- An electrical fault, such as a tripped circuit or a damaged cord, plug or outlet: stop using the appliance; do not work on electrical components (Do not perform entry 1; 02 C-CL06-02).
- Occupants or pets cannot be kept off a wet floor: do not mop that area until they can be (02 C-CL06-02).
- A sharp or biohazard item in a bin, linens or clutter: stop and report (04 CL-06 "hazards", ASSUMPTION).
- The residence cannot be secured as the customer instructed at closeout: escalate before leaving, unless your own safety requires you to leave (CL-06.QAC "secure closeout"; 31 section 8.4 rule 3).

- [ ] **Uniform Hanging:** Hang clothing items uniformly inside the wardrobe (all hangers facing the same direction, items spaced evenly).
- [ ] **Shoe Presentation:** Align shoes neatly on the closet floor or shoe racks, grouped by style and color, facing forward.

> FLAG (C-CL06-V01): tier scope undefined - pricing reserves closet organization and garment steaming for the Gold tier and above, while the steps of this phase make no tier distinction and the agreement gives the same scope to every tier. > FLAG (C-CL06-V01, C-CL06-12): tier scope undefined - pricing reserves "ambient scent refreshing" for the Gold tier and above, while the step above runs on every visit; the per-tier task list is an owner decision (Q-28-21). ## 19. Escalation

> 1. Stop the affected work and keep yourself, occupants and pets away from the hazard; do not try to fix, move or disturb it (source: spec START-011; risk-triage playbook L7).
> 2. Emergency (fire, gas odor, injury, electrical contact, structural movement): call emergency services first, then `<owner decision: emergency contact>` (source: spec BRF-013).
> 3. Only if policy allows and it is safe, take the minimum action needed to prevent immediate injury or property damage, and record why and what you did (source: spec JOB-006).
> 4. Report in the app with Stop Work / Report Hazard: type, photos and severity (source: spec JOB-004, UXE-004). Before the job has started, record No Start / Blocked instead of starting (source: spec SCH-008, AT-030).
> 5. Escalate with one tap to `<owner decision: escalation order>`; the item enters the owner/supervisor queue (source: spec UXE-002, JOB-012).
> 6. Wait for release by the role named in the hold, with evidence recorded. Critical holds clear only with a recorded qualified assessment (source: owner facts 2026-10-02; 27 Roles and permissions section 6.3).
> 7. Tell the customer only what the approved "work blocked" message says; never send incident details or access codes by text or email (source: spec NTF-007, COM-002).
> 8. Resume only after the briefing is updated and re-acknowledged, with a change order if scope or price changes (source: spec START-002, FIN-008, JOB-005).

Service roles (contacts are owner decisions, Q-31-05):

- Garment care or surface finish questions: owner, from the care labels and the manufacturer's care instructions.
- Chemical or fragrance reactions: emergency services first where needed, then the qualified safety professional.
- Electrical faults: stop using the appliance; a licensed electrician through the owner.
- Lone-work concerns: owner (check-in protocol TBD).
- Requests beyond the plan's tasks (washing, errands, fridge, mail and packages): owner, by change order once a reviewed step exists.

## 20. Incident reporting

> - Report every injury, property damage, near miss, customer conflict, tool or equipment failure and suspected hazardous-material exposure on the incident form (source: spec JOB-008).
> - An injured person comes first: call emergency services; give only the first aid that the company's approved first-response arrangements cover (not yet defined; pending qualified safety review); medical decisions belong to emergency services and medical professionals (source: master section 1.3; safety-liability-manager skill "prioritize immediate safety and emergency response over paperwork").
> - Record facts, photos and actions taken. Do not guess causes or discuss fault or liability with the customer; refer those questions to the owner (source: spec AIR-012 boundary "liability admissions require human review").
> - Keep injury and medical details out of customer-visible notes and messages (source: spec PERM-003, COM-002).
> - Report as soon as it is safe and before the job is closed (source: spec Appendix A APXA-007). High-severity incidents go to the escalation queue (source: spec AT-035, JOB-012).

**HUMAN REVIEW:** attorney licensed in KY and/or IN for the fault and liability sentence; insurance broker for any insurer-notification step added later.

Examples for this service, all reported under JOB-008 (source: spec CL-06.HAZ; 02 C-CL06-02): a steam or iron burn; a slip on a damp floor; a skin, eye or breathing reaction to a product or fragrance; damage to a garment, finish or high-value item; a missing item or valuables concern; a lost key or an access code exposed outside the app (privacy owner); equipment failure.
